# Professional story — from diagnostics to software systems

My path into software engineering began with a hands-on interest in how technology works and how to diagnose it when it fails. Hardware and device troubleshooting taught me to separate symptoms from causes, test assumptions, and verify changes rather than rely on guesswork.

I later worked across practical operations and smartphone hardware/software diagnostics, and also taught device-repair concepts. Those experiences made it clear that the same systematic thinking can be applied beyond an individual repair: scripts and software can make repeated processes easier to perform and inspect.

A recurring task at an institute became an early automation exercise: using Python and OCR tooling to extract information from screenshots into a structured file. The task was small, but it demonstrated the value of converting manual steps into a repeatable workflow.

That experience helped direct my focus toward full-stack applications, APIs, databases, and automation. I now work on web, Android, desktop, and workflow-oriented projects, with an emphasis on clear data flows, practical problem-solving, evidence-led debugging, and maintainable implementations.

## The through-line

- **Diagnose:** identify symptoms, isolate causes, and verify the fix.
- **Automate:** make recurring steps explicit and repeatable.
- **Build:** connect interfaces, logic, and data into useful software.
- **Validate:** document what works, what remains uncertain, and what needs further testing.
